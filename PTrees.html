PTrees

Simulating pedigree structure and genetic panels at scale.

Software overview

PTrees generates familial structure from a handful of parameters set in a configuration file: the number of families, the number of generations, the probability of reproduction, and the maximum and minimum number of offspring. For an example, see config.txt in the PTrees repository. The tree structures themselves are simulated in Java. A few examples follow.

This first example shows a familial structure with 8 generations. The configuration file requested 10, but because reproduction is probabilistic the parameter only caps growth rather than guaranteeing it. This is a simple one-family tree in which all individuals are related.

The second example shows three separate families of varying height and relationship. You can imagine how noisy a relationship matrix for this population would be. In the family at the bottom right, the leaf individuals share only 0.0195% of their genetic material — distinguishing that from the estimated relationship between unrelated individuals is a genuinely hard task, which is exactly why treelet covariance smoothers are interesting.

This third example is more confusing still. Several unrelated individuals are introduced to mask the distant relationships further. It is also much closer to what a practitioner actually sees: a number of unrelated people with little known structure.

Generating genetic panels

Once the pedigree structure is generated and the number of SNPs is specified, PTrees follows a simple probabilistic method to assign genetic panels. By default, SNPs are evenly placed across the first 22 chromosomes.

PTrees first randomly generates minor allele frequencies (MAF) between 0.05 and 0.5 for each sample on the genome. It then walks the pedigree: if an individual is a founder, it assigns a minor allele count (MAC) by sampling from a binomial with probability equal to the MAF. If an individual has both parents in the pedigree, PTrees chooses the MAC with equal probability from the mother and father and assigns that value to the individual’s panel.

This is in no way a realistic representation of how genetic information actually passes through a pedigree. That problem is both much harder to simulate in general and specific to a population. PTrees makes some strong assumptions, and in exchange the user gets a fast, easy way to produce familial structures with associated genetic information at scale.

From there PTrees uses Plink to format bed, bim, and fam files. A simple GCTA call — not implemented here — then produces an estimated relationship matrix that can be compared against the known relationships in the generated pedigree.
